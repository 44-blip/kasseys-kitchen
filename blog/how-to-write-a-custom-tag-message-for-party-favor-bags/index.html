How to Write a Custom Tag Message for Party Favor Bags

Published November 11, 2026

If you are planning cookie favor bags, the tag is the part guests read first. Knowing how to write a custom tag message for party favor bags comes down to three choices: who it is from, how short it is, and what tone fits your event. This guide walks through each one with real wording you can borrow, then shows how the tag works on Kassey's Kitchen bags.

How to write a custom tag message for party favor bags: the short answer

Write one or two short lines. Say thank you, name the occasion or the hosts, and stop. A good tag reads in about three seconds, sounds like you, and works even if a guest never opens the bag until the next day.

A simple formula works for almost any event:

  1. A thank-you or a toast. "Thank you for celebrating with us."
  2. The occasion or a small detail. "Maya's 30th" or "Baby Lee is on the way."
  3. A sign-off. "Love, Maya and Dan."

You do not need all three. Two is often better than three.

Start with who the message is from and who it is for

Before you write a word, decide the voice. A tag from "us" sounds different than one from a single host or from a group.

  • From a couple or family: "From our family to yours." This works for birthdays, holidays and anniversaries.
  • From one host: "Thank you for being here. Love, Priya." Personal and warm.
  • From the guest of honor: "Thanks for sharing my day. Sofia." Good for graduations and quinceañeras.
  • From a company or team: "Happy holidays from all of us at the front desk." Good for office gatherings.

Then think about the reader. A bag handed to a coworker needs a different line than one handed to your grandmother. If the guest list is mixed, aim for the person who knows you least. A clear thank-you never misses.

Keep it short enough to fit a small tag

A tag on a favor bag is small. That is the whole point of it. Long messages get cramped, and they pull attention away from the cookies.

Use these rules of thumb:

  • Aim for one sentence, or two very short ones.
  • Cut any word that does not change the meaning. "We are so very happy and grateful" becomes "Thank you."
  • Skip long dates and full addresses.
  • Read it out loud. If you run out of breath, trim it.

Match the tone to the event

The same cookies can feel playful at a kid's party and elegant at a wedding shower. The tag sets that tone.

  • Playful: short, a little joke, maybe a pun on sweet or cookie.
  • Warm: plain thanks, first names, no jokes.
  • Polished: full sentences, no exclamation points, last names or a formal sign-off.

If your dessert table has a theme, echo one word from it on the tag. That ties the bag to the rest of the party. Our guide to planning a dessert table for a graduation party covers theme and color choices that make this easy.

Tag message ideas for showers, birthdays, graduations and holidays

Use these as starting points and change the names, the occasion and the sign-off.

Baby shower

  • "Sweet news is on the way. Thank you for celebrating with us."
  • "Thanks for showering us with love. Baby Lee's family."

Bridal shower

  • "Thank you for celebrating Ana with us."
  • "She said yes, and we said cookies. Thank you for being here."

Birthday

  • "Thanks for helping me celebrate. Love, Daniel."
  • "Another year sweeter. Thank you for coming."

Graduation

  • "Thank you for helping me get here. Class of [year]."
  • "I did it, and you were part of it. Thank you."

Quinceañera

  • "Gracias por celebrar conmigo. Love, Valeria." A line in Spanish, or a mix of English and Spanish, fits well if that is how your family talks.

Holidays and office gatherings

  • "Happy holidays from our family to yours."
  • "Thank you for a great year. Happy holidays from the team."

Details to leave off the tag

Some things look fine in a draft and cause trouble later.

  • Allergy claims. Do not write anything on the tag that promises a bag is safe for a specific diet. Kassey's Kitchen bakes in a home kitchen that handles nuts, dairy, wheat and eggs, so allergen-free products cannot be guaranteed. Tell her about allergies in your request instead.
  • Health claims. Cookies are a treat. Keep the tag about the occasion.
  • Inside jokes. A joke only half the room understands can leave the other half out.
  • Too many names. A list of ten names will not fit and someone will be missed. Use "from all of us" or "the Lee family."
  • Private details. Skip phone numbers and home addresses. Guests do not need them on a cookie bag.
  • Anything you have not double-checked. Spell names and dates carefully before you send them.
